# Energy Management

## Question 1: Activities That Make Me Relax (Calm Quadrant)

* Listening to calm music.
* Taking a short walk.
* Doing light stretching or yoga.
* Meditating or focusing on my breath.
* Reading something light or motivational.
* Taking a warm bath.
* Spending time in nature.

## Question 2: When I Get Into the Stress Quadrant

* When I have many deadlines close together.
* When I am learning something new and difficult.
* When I feel I am not making enough progress.
* When I have too many tasks and not enough time.
* When I worry about my performance or results.

## Question 3: How I Know I Am in the Excitement Quadrant

* I feel energetic and motivated.
* I enjoy what I am doing and look forward to it.
* I can focus easily without feeling tired.
* I feel positive and hopeful about my work.
* I do not feel stressed, but I am still active and engaged.

## Question 4: Sleep Is Your Superpower (Brief Points)

* Sleep is essential for brain and body health.
* It helps with memory, learning, and focus.
* Lack of sleep reduces learning ability and weakens immunity.
* Good sleep lowers the risk of diseases like Alzheimer’s and heart problems.
* Sleep helps control emotions and reduces anxiety and depression.
* A regular sleep schedule improves overall health and energy.

## Question 5: Ideas to Sleep Better

* Go to bed and wake up at the same time every day.
* Keep the bedroom dark, quiet, and cool.
* Avoid screens and blue light before bedtime.
* Avoid caffeine and heavy meals late in the evening.
* Do a relaxing activity before sleep, like reading or light stretching.
* Track my sleep and improve my routine gradually.

## Question 6: Brain-Changing Benefits of Exercise (Minimum 5 Points)

* Exercise improves mood by increasing dopamine, serotonin, and endorphins.
* It enhances focus, attention, and reaction time immediately after a workout.
* Regular exercise grows new brain cells in the hippocampus and improves memory.
* It reduces stress, anxiety, and risk of depression.
* Exercise protects the brain from aging and diseases like Alzheimer’s.
* It strengthens the prefrontal cortex, which helps with planning and emotional control.

## Question 7: Steps to Exercise More

* Start with short sessions, like 10–15 minutes a day.
* Choose activities I enjoy, such as walking, cycling, or dancing.
* Schedule exercise at the same time every day.
* Use tiny habits, like doing two push-ups after brushing my teeth.
* Track my workouts and celebrate small progress.
* Exercise with a friend or join a group for motivation.
* Remind myself of the mental and brain benefits, not only physical ones.
